This is one of the two [integration models](/partner-api/v2/integration-models), `payment_mode: "partner"` in the API (also called partner settlement). Choose **Non-crypto model** in Get started when your customers have no crypto wallet. You buy for them and charge them however you already do. We keep each card in a CatchBack vault for that customer, and everything they do (orders, sell-backs, shipping) goes on one statement between you and us.

|  | Crypto Wallet model | Non-crypto model |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays CatchBack | Each customer, in USDC | You, on your statement |
| A customer is | A wallet address | Your own id for them |
| Their cards | NFTs in their wallet | In their CatchBack vault, read with the API |
| Sell-backs | USDC to their wallet | Credited to your statement |
| Shipping | Paid by the holder | On your statement |
| Revenue share | Paid to your USDC address | Netted on your statement |

The endpoints for catalog, odds, orders, webhooks and keys are the same. Wallet fields (`recipient`, `refund_address`, `partner_user_ref`, `holder`, `payout_chain`, `payout_address`) are refused: an order, a shipment estimate or a shipment that sends any of them answers `400 WALLET_FIELDS_NOT_ACCEPTED`, with `fields` naming them. Token ids, quotes and burns aren't used: the wallet, token, quote and shipment endpoints answer `400 CRYPTO_WALLET_MODEL_ONLY`.

## Your customers

Every call names a customer by `customer`: your id for them, 1-128 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ . : -`, not dots alone. Use something stable that is not personal data (your internal user id, never an email address or phone number).

Ids are trimmed of leading and trailing spaces, and are case-sensitive: `User-1842` and `user-1842` are two customers. The first order for a new id creates that customer's CatchBack account (one refused for your credit limit or a daily limit creates none); later orders with the same id reach the same account. Ids are yours alone: the same id in another partner's program is a different customer. You never see or send our account ids.

## Buy a pack: `POST /orders`

```bash
curl -X POST "$CATCHBACK_BASE_URL/api/partner/v2/orders" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $CATCHBACK_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: order-7f3a91" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"pack_type":"bronze","category":"pokemon","odds":"standard","quantity":2,"customer":"user-1842"}'
```

Use a pack that is selling: one with `available: true` from [List packs](/partner-api/v2/endpoints/list-packs), and for a pack that sells the odds choice, odds that are `available` on its [pool](/partner-api/v2/endpoints/get-pack-pool).

There is no payment step: the order is charged to your statement when it is accepted, and its packs open right away. The response is the [order](/partner-api/v2/orders) with `custody: "vault"` and `customer`, and each pack's status ends at `in_vault` instead of `minted`. A retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` returns the same order and is never charged twice. Order keys are per program: one key is one order, whichever customer it names (the same key with a different `customer` is `422 IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`).

An order is refused, and nothing is charged, when:

| Status | Code | When |
|---|---|---|
| `402` | `CREDIT_LIMIT` | It would take what you owe past your credit limit. The error carries `owed`, `credit_limit` and `order_total`. |
| `429` | `CUSTOMER_DAILY_LIMIT` | This customer has spent their limit for the UTC day. Carries `spent_today`, `limit` and `retry_after_seconds`. |
| `429` | `DAILY_LIMIT` | Your program has spent its daily limit, if it has one. Same fields. |
| `403` | `CUSTOMER_BLOCKED` | We can't sell to this customer. Don't retry. |
| `400` | `BAD_CUSTOMER` | `customer` is missing or not 1-128 of the allowed characters. |
| `400` | `WALLET_FIELDS_NOT_ACCEPTED` | A wallet field was sent. Send `customer` only. |

The daily limits count every order accepted that UTC day. A pack that was accepted but couldn't open still counts, even though its price comes back off your statement as an `order_refund`.

## Their vault: `GET /customers/{customer}/vault`

The cards the customer holds now, newest first, each with what it sells back for at this moment:

```json
{
  "customer": "user-1842",
  "cards": [
    {
      "card": { "id": "838a5e7f-…", "category": "pokemon", "name": "Charizard ex PSA 10" },
      "order_id": "order-7f3a91",
      "pulled_at": "2026-11-24T18:02:11Z",
      "value_at_pull": 31,
      "sellback": { "amount": 31, "rate": 1, "fresh": true, "fresh_until": "2026-11-24T18:07:11Z", "locked_until": "2026-12-01T18:02:11Z" }
    }
  ],
  "truncated": false
}
```

`sellback.fresh` is true while the card is in its fresh window after the pull, at the higher rate; `fresh_until` is when that window ends (5 minutes after `pulled_at`). `card.id` is the same id as the item in the pack's pool, so its image and grade are on [Get a pack's odds and pool](/partner-api/v2/endpoints/get-pack-pool). For 7 days after a card is acquired its sell-back is based on its value when acquired (`locked_until`), so a price change in that time doesn't move the offer. A sold or shipped card leaves the vault. `truncated: true` means the customer has more pulls than one read covers. A customer id you have never sold to returns an empty vault (`200`), not a `404`; the shipment endpoints answer `404 CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` for one.

## Sell back: `POST /customers/{customer}/cards/{card_id}/sellback`

```bash
curl -X POST "$CATCHBACK_BASE_URL/api/partner/v2/customers/user-1842/cards/838a5e7f-…/sellback" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $CATCHBACK_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"min_payout": 30}'
```

Sells the card at its current offer and credits the payout to your statement; nothing is paid out to the customer, so you credit them your way. `min_payout` (optional) refuses an offer below it (`409 PRICE_CHANGED`). A card sells once: a retry, even one sent at the same moment as the first, returns the first sale with `status: "already_sold"`, so no `Idempotency-Key` is needed. A card id that is a UUID matches in any case. A card your program didn't pull into this vault is `404 CARD_NOT_IN_VAULT`. The sale's own refusals keep their code (`CARD_IN_BATTLE`, `CARD_LISTED_ON_MARKETPLACE`, `SHOP_PURCHASE_NOT_SELLABLE`, `GOODS_NOT_SELLABLE`, `NO_PURCHASE_RECORD`); any other, such as a card that was shipped or is on its way out, is `SELLBACK_REFUSED` (usually `400`), and the message says why.

```json
{ "sellback": { "status": "sold", "transaction_id": "…", "payout_amount": 31, "payout_rate": 1, "on_statement": true } }
```

## Ship: estimate, then create

1. [`POST /customers/{customer}/shipments/estimate`](#ship-estimate-then-create) with 1-10 `card_ids` (from the vault, one box) and an `address` returns `rates`, each with a `carrier`, `service`, `token` and `amount`. Add `cover_amount` to price shipping insurance: each rate then also has a `total`, the postage plus the cover's premium.
2. `POST /customers/{customer}/shipments` with the same `card_ids` and `address`, the chosen `rate: { carrier, token }`, and `max_cost` (that rate's `total` if you sent `cover_amount`, else its `amount`), plus an `Idempotency-Key` (shipment keys are per program: the same key for another customer is `422 IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`). The postage (and any cover) goes on your statement; past your credit limit it is refused with `402 CREDIT_LIMIT` (with `owed`, `credit_limit` and `shipment_total`). Each key can create up to 50 shipments a UTC day (then `429 DAILY_SHIPMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`, with `retry_after_seconds`). The response is the shipment, with its `id`.
3. Follow it with [`nft_shipment.updated`](/partner-api/v2/webhooks#events) or `GET /customers/{customer}/shipments` (the customer's latest 100, newest first). `status` moves `processing` → `shipped` (with `carrier` and `tracking_number`) → `delivered`. The first webhook, at `processing`, comes when the shipment is created.

A rate that rose above `max_cost` since the estimate is refused with `PRICE_CHANGED`. A card refusal has one of two codes:

- `CARDS_NOT_IN_VAULT`: your program never pulled that card into this customer's vault (another customer's card, or a wrong id). `card_ids` lists them.
- `CARDS_NOT_SHIPPABLE`: the card was in this vault but can't ship now: sold back, already shipping, listed or on hold. `cards` gives each one's `reason`, except when a card left the vault while the shipment was being created.

A shipment that doesn't go through is taken off your statement.

## Your statement: `GET /settlement`

What you owe now, your limits, and one month of entries (`period=YYYY-MM`, the current UTC month by default):

```json
{
  "owed": 236.65,
  "credit_limit": 10000,
  "available": 9763.35,
  "customer_daily_limit": 500,
  "daily_limit": null,
  "period": {
    "month": "2026-11", "from": "2026-11-01T00:00:00.000Z", "to": "2026-12-01T00:00:00.000Z",
    "opening_balance": 0, "closing_balance": 236.65,
    "totals": { "order": { "amount": 250, "entries": 2 }, "sellback": { "amount": -31, "entries": 1 }, "shipping": { "amount": 17.65, "entries": 1 } }
  },
  "entries": [
    { "id": "…", "kind": "sellback", "amount": -31, "created_at": "…", "customer": "user-1842", "order_id": null, "card_id": "838a5e7f-…", "sellback_id": "…" }
  ],
  "next_cursor": null
}
```

A positive `amount` adds to what you owe, a negative one takes from it. The kinds are `order`, `order_refund`, `shipping`, `shipping_refund`, `sellback`, `revenue_share`, `payment` and `adjustment`. Page with `limit` (up to 100, default 50) and `cursor`. Your manager's **Statement** page shows the same, with a CSV export.

Your revenue share is netted on the statement, and you settle the balance as agreed with us. Your credit limit and daily limits are set by CatchBack; ask your contact to change them.

## Webhooks

The events are the same as the Crypto Wallet model's, with `customer` instead of a wallet:

- `nft_order.updated` as an order's packs open into the vault (`in_vault`), or are `refunded`.
- `sellback.updated` when a sell-back is credited (`status: "credited"`, `amount`, `card_id`, `statement_entry_id`).
- `nft_shipment.updated` when a shipment is created (`processing`) and as it moves.

They're picked up every two minutes or so. In the sandbox, `POST /sandbox/tick` sends them at once.

## In the sandbox

Get started's guide for this model runs every step against the sandbox: an order for a test customer, the vault, a sell-back, the statement, and a shipment. In the sandbox you play our shipping team with `POST /sandbox/shipments/{id}` and `{"status":"shipped"}` or `{"status":"delivered"}`, using the shipment's `id`. The sandbox's one shipping rate has no insurance, so leave `cover_amount` out there.
